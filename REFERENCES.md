# References

Master bibliography for the *Harness Engineering* series, grouped by post. Where a source is referenced from multiple posts, it is listed once under the post it primarily belongs to (its topical home), and other posts cross-link to that entry — so a source may be *cited* in an earlier post than the one it is *filed* under. URLs are given where a stable canonical URL exists; living vendor docs cite the documentation root.

> **How to read this file.** A **bold** bullet is a master entry: the full citation, filed once, in the section of the post that owns it. A plain bullet is a cross-reference: `Surname, "Title" — see Post NN`. Together the bold bullets are the master list; nothing is listed twice. Citation style is author(s) or org, title, venue or publisher, year, with a one-line note explaining which point the source supports where that is not obvious. Sources shared with the *Context Engineering* series are marked **[CE]** and cite that series' `REFERENCES.md` for the full entry.

> **Two works by one author under two mastheads count as one source.** Osmani's "Agent Harness Engineering" (personal blog and O'Reilly Radar) is a single entry, as is his "Loop engineering". Where a publisher has two genuinely different articles — MindStudio's harness explainer and its Stripe throughput piece — they are two entries, and each says so.

## Post 04 — Why the harness beats the model

- **Zhang, Y., Wang, J., Ge, Y., Xu, W., Hamm, J., and Reddy, C. K.** "Stop Comparing LLM Agents Without Disclosing the Harness." *arXiv:2605.23950*, 7 May 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23950 — the Binding Constraint Thesis: for long-horizon tasks across models of comparable frontier capability, "performance variance is governed more by harness configuration than by model choice, and current evaluation protocols therefore systematically misattribute harness-level gains to model improvements". A control-theoretic formalisation of the harness as controller, evidence that harness-induced variance can exceed model-induced variance including model ranking reversal, and a disclosure standard with a variance-decomposition protocol.
- **Starace, J.** "Scaffold Effects on GAIA: A Controlled Comparison." *arXiv:2606.08529*, 7 June 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08529 — a pre-registered controlled comparison of three scaffolds (ReAct, planner-actor-rater, planner-then-executor) across five models from three providers on GAIA validation Levels 1–2, tasks and conditions held fixed, three attempts per question. "Scaffold choice alone moves measured accuracy by as much as 28 percentage points within a single model (Opus, Level 2, robust slice)"; the source of the term *elicitation gap* and of the finding that it "is not guaranteed to shrink as models improve".
- **Guo, J., et al.** "From Question Answering to Task Completion: A Survey on Agent System and Harness Design." *arXiv:2606.20683*, 14 June 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20683 — the model-harness lens: whether the bottleneck sits in the foundation model, the execution harness, or the coupling between them; the harness decomposed into six runtime responsibilities (observation, context, control, action, state, verification).
- **Ding, F., Zhang, Y., Liu, R., Liao, Y., Zeng, Z., and Yang, H.** "Scaffold-Mediated Post-Training: Co-Evolving Model Parameters and Procedural Scaffold Graphs." *arXiv:2608.05156*, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05156 — the co-training flywheel run as an explicit training procedure (discovery, distillation, dynamic recompilation); on FeatureBench, discovered skills improve the passed rate by 8.1pp and the distilled model retains a 27.7% passed rate with no external scaffold, an 85.2% distillation retention rate. The year only is given: the arXiv identifier and the reported submission date disagree.
- **Trivedy, V., and Daugherty, M.** "Tuning Deep Agents to Work Well with Different Models." *LangChain blog*, 29 April 2026. https://www.langchain.com/blog/tuning-deep-agents-different-models — "A single harness can't be optimal for every model"; per-model profiles moved τ²-bench from 33% to 53% for GPT-5.3-Codex and from 43% to 53% for Claude Opus 4.7. The evidence that harness gains are model-specific and do not transfer.
- **MindStudio.** "What Is Harness Engineering? Why Your Agent Wrapper Drives More Performance Than the Model." *MindStudio blog*, 2026. https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/what-is-harness-engineering — "the same underlying model can produce performance gaps of up to 6x depending on how the agent wrapper — the harness — is designed". This article carries the spread figure only; MindStudio's Stripe throughput numbers are in a separate article, filed under Post 17.

## Post 21 — Observability & trace-driven repair

- **OpenTelemetry.** "Semantic conventions for generative AI systems." *OpenTelemetry documentation*, 2025–26. https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/gen-ai/ — the standard span and attribute names (`gen_ai.usage.*`, model and agent spans) so traces render in any compatible backend. - **W3C.** "Trace Context." *W3C Recommendation*, 23 November 2021. https://www.w3.org/TR/trace-context/ — the `traceparent` HTTP header, which "identifies the incoming request in a tracing system" and "has four fields: version, trace-id, parent-id, trace-flags". ## Post 22 — Evaluating harnesses

- **Terminal-Bench.** Agentic terminal-task benchmark, a joint project of Stanford University and the Laude Institute; version 2.0 released 7 November 2025. https://www.tbench.ai/ — 89 hard, human-verified tasks, each run in its own container under the Harbor runner with a human-written oracle solution and tests that verify final container state, and deliberately calibrated to keep frontier models under a 50% resolve rate. The benchmark this series' headline harness-only result is measured on. Launch write-up: Snorkel AI, "Terminal-Bench 2.0: raising the bar for AI agent evaluation," https://snorkel.ai/blog/terminal-bench-2-0-raising-the-bar-for-ai-agent-evaluation/.
- **Jimenez, C., Yang, J., et al.** "SWE-bench: Can Language Models Resolve Real-World GitHub Issues?" *ICLR 2024* (arXiv:2310.06770). https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770 — the original real-repository task set, graded on hidden tests the agent never sees.
- **Yang, J., Jimenez, C., et al.** "SWE-agent: Agent-Computer Interfaces Enable Automated Software Engineering." *NeurIPS 2024* (arXiv:2405.15793). https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15793 — how the agent-computer interface (the harness) around a fixed model moves resolved-rate.
- **OpenAI.** "Introducing SWE-bench Verified." *OpenAI*, 13 August 2024. https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/ — a 500-instance human-validated subset of SWE-bench, filtered by expert software engineers from 1,699 reviewed problems with three reviewers each, after under-specified issue statements and over-specific tests were found in the original.
- **OpenAI.** "Why SWE-bench Verified no longer measures frontier coding capabilities." *OpenAI*, February 2026. https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/ — OpenAI stops reporting SWE-bench Verified and recommends others do the same, citing contamination and an audit of 138 hard tasks that found material problems in the test design or problem statement in 59.4% of them; recommends SWE-bench Pro instead. The page returns HTTP 403 to automated fetchers; these two figures are corroborated across independent write-ups.
- **Deng, X., Da, J., et al.** "SWE-Bench Pro: Can AI Agents Solve Long-Horizon Software Engineering Tasks?" 2025 (arXiv:2509.16941). https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16941 — 1,865 long-horizon problems over 41 actively maintained repositories, split into a public set (11 repositories), a held-out set (12) and a commercial set (18) as a contamination-resistant testbed.
- **Yu, B., Zhu, Y., He, P., Kang, D.** "UTBoost: Rigorous Evaluation of Coding Agents on SWE-Bench." *ACL 2025* (arXiv:2506.09289). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09289 — test augmentation found 36 SWE-bench task instances with insufficient test cases and 345 erroneous patches incorrectly labelled as passing; corrections affected 40.9% of SWE-bench Lite and 24.4% of SWE-bench Verified leaderboard entries, yielding 18 and 11 ranking changes.
- **Yao, S., Shinn, N., Razavi, P., Narasimhan, K.** "τ-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains." 2024 (arXiv:2406.12045). https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045 — proposes pass^k, the probability that all k trials succeed, as the reliability counterpart to pass@k; state-of-the-art function-calling agents succeed on under 50% of tasks and fall below 25% at pass^8 in the retail domain.
- **Miller, E.** "Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations." 2024 (arXiv:2411.00640). https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640 — five recommendations for eval statistics, including clustered standard errors when questions come in related groups (they can exceed naive ones by more than 3×) and inference on question-level paired differences rather than population-level summary statistics when two models are compared.
- **Strathern, M.** "'Improving ratings': audit in the British University system." *European Review* 5(3), 1997, pp. 305–321. https://doi.org/10.1017/S1062798700002660 — the source of the widely quoted formulation "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure", restating Goodhart's 1975 observation about monetary aggregates.
- **GTFOBins.** Curated list of Unix binaries usable to bypass local security restrictions, 2026. https://gtfobins.org/ — "a curated list of Unix binaries that can be used to bypass local security restrictions in misconfigured systems".
